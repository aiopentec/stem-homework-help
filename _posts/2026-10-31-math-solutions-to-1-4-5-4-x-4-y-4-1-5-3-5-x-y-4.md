### 1. Restatement of the Problem in Plain Language

We are investigating a specific Diophantine (integer/rational) equation of the form:
$$1^4 + 5^4 + x^4 + y^4 = \left(1 + 5^3(5 + x + y)\right)^4$$

By substituting $x = 5(p+q)$ and $y = 5(p-q)$, the problem reduces to finding rational points $(p, q)$ that satisfy certain quadratic relations involving a square root. Through algebraic manipulation, the search for rational solutions is transformed into the problem of finding rational values of $r$ that make two specific quartics, denoted $F_1$ and $F_2$, equal to the square of a rational number ($q^2$).

Known historical solutions (such as those by Vepir) yield gigantic rational numbers for $r$ with upwards of 80 to over 168 digits, corresponding to massive integer solutions. 

**The Question:** Are there other rational numbers $r$ that make $F_1$ or $F_2$ squares, but have **substantially smaller heights** (i.e., smaller numerators and denominators with fewer digits) than the known solutions?

### 4. Common Mistakes

* **Confusing the Quartic with a Simple Polynomial:** Trying to factor $F_1$ or $F_2$ directly using high-school algebra. These are curves of genus 1 (elliptic curves), not polynomials that factor over rational roots easily.
* **Ignoring the Denominator $m$:** Forgetting that $F_1$ and $F_2$ are divided by $m^2$, meaning the condition is strictly that the *numerator* must equal a square times $m^2$ (or equivalently, the full expression is a square of a rational).
