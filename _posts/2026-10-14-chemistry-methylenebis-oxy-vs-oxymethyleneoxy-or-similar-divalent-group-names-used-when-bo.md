## 1. What the question is asking (in plain language)

The student wants to know:

* When we have the –O‑CH₂‑O‑ fragment (two oxygen atoms linked by a methylene carbon), can we call it **“methylenebis(oxy)”** only when the fragment bridges **two different parts of the same molecule** (e.g. a 1,2‑substituted benzene)?
* Or can the same fragment also be called **“oxymethyleneoxy”** (or a similar name) when it is attached **once** to a single parent structure?
* In other words – what are the IUPAC‑approved ways of naming this fragment in the two different situations, and why does the Wikipedia entry *1,2‑[methylenebis(oxy)]benzene* look acceptable?

---

## 2. Step‑by‑step answer  

### Step 1 – Identify the fragment and the two possible connection patterns  

```
   O–CH2–O
```

* **Bridging (bidentate) connection** – the two O atoms are each bonded to a different atom of the *parent* (e.g. two adjacent carbons of a benzene ring).  
* **Monodentate (substituent) connection** – only one of the O atoms is bonded to the parent; the other O is “inside” the substituent.

The IUPAC “Blue Book” treats these two cases differently.

---

### Step 2 – IUPAC name for the **bridging** case  

**Rule** (Blue Book 2013, P‑15.3.1.2.2.1):  
When the same *multiplicative* group occurs more than once in a *single* substituent, the group is written once and preceded by a **multiplicative prefix** (bis, tris, …). The whole group is placed in parentheses and the **locants of the attachment points** are written before the parentheses.

*The fragment –O‑CH₂‑O‑ is the *bis(oxy)* of a methylene bridge, therefore the systematic name of the fragment itself is **methylenebis(oxy)**.*

**Putting it on a parent:**  

```
parent‑locant1,parent‑locant2‑(methylenebis(oxy))
```

Example – benzene with the fragment joining C‑1 and C‑2:

```
1,2-(methylenebis(oxy))benzene
```

The same structure is also known as the fused‑ring system **1,2‑benzodioxole** (the preferred IUPAC name for the heterocycle). Both names are *correct*; the “‑(methylenebis(oxy))‑” version follows the substitutive‑nomenclature rules and is therefore allowed.

---

### Step 3 – IUPAC name for the **monodentate** (single‑attachment) case  

When the fragment is attached through **one** of its oxygens, the IUPAC “substituent” name is built from the *parent* of the fragment (oxymethylene) and the atom by which it is attached (oxygen). The result is the **oxymethyleneoxy** prefix.

* Construction:

| fragment part | IUPAC term | Reason |
|--------------|------------|--------|
| –CH₂– attached to O | **oxymethylene** | carbon attached to oxygen, the carbon bearing the two oxygens is the “oxymethylene” unit |
| the external O that bonds to the parent | **‑oxy** | indicates that the point of attachment is an oxygen atom |

Thus the whole substituent is **oxymethyleneoxy‑**.

**Putting it on a parent:**  

```
parent‑locant‑(oxymethyleneoxy)
```

Example – a C₆₀ fullerene bearing the fragment through one oxygen:

```
1,9-(oxymethyleneoxy)[C60‑Ih][5,6]fullerene
```

Here the locants (1,9) refer to the two carbon atoms of the fullerene that are linked **indirectly** through the oxygen of the substituent; the fragment itself is attached only once (through an O).

---

### Step 4 – Why the two names are not interchangeable  

| Situation | Correct prefix | Reason |
|-----------|----------------|--------|
| **Bridging** (two separate attachment points on the *same* parent) | **methylenebis(oxy)** | The multiplicative prefix “bis” tells the reader that the same –O‑CH₂‑O‑ unit is used twice, each time to connect a different atom of the parent. |
| **Monodentate** (only one attachment to the parent) | **oxymethyleneoxy** | No multiplicative prefix is needed; the name describes the *internal* constitution of the substituent (oxymethylene) and the atom that makes the bond to the parent (oxy). |

If you used “methylenebis(oxy)” for a monodentate substituent, the name would incorrectly suggest a second attachment point that does not exist. Conversely, using “oxymethyleneoxy” for a bridging fragment would hide the fact that the fragment links two positions.

---

### Step 5 – Summary of the answer to the original question  

* **Yes**, “1,2‑[methylenebis(oxy)]benzene” (or more formally **1,2-(methylenebis(oxy))benzene**) is a perfectly valid IUPAC name for the benzodioxole ring when the –O‑CH₂‑O‑ group bridges two adjacent carbons of benzene.  
* The name **“oxymethyleneoxy”** is **not** a synonym for “methylenebis(oxy)”; it is the *monodentate* substituent name that is used **only when the fragment is attached through a single oxygen atom**.  
* Therefore, “methylenebis(oxy)” is *suitable only for bridging (bidentate) situations*, whereas “oxymethyleneoxy” (or analogous constructions) is used when the fragment is a *single‑attachment substituent*.

---

## 3. Final answer (concise)

* **Bridging (two‑point) attachment** → **methylenebis(oxy)**, written as *locant‑(methylenebis(oxy))* (e.g. **1,2-(methylenebis(oxy))benzene**).  
* **Monodentate (single‑point) attachment** → **oxymethyleneoxy**, written as *locant‑(oxymethyleneoxy)* (e.g. **1,9-(oxymethyleneoxy)[C₆₀‑Ih][5,6]fullerene**).  

Thus, “methylenebis(oxy)” is not used for a substituent that is bound to only one moiety; for that case the correct IUPAC prefix is “oxymethyleneoxy”.

---

## 4. Common mistakes to avoid  

| Mistake | Why it’s wrong | How to fix it |
|---------|----------------|--------------|
| **Using “methylenebis(oxy)” for a monodentate substituent** | “bis” implies two identical attachment points; the name would suggest a second, non‑existent bond. | Use the monodentate prefix **oxymethyleneoxy** (or another appropriate substituent name). |
| **Omitting the parentheses** (e.g. writing “1,2‑methylenebis(oxy)benzene”) | The parentheses signal that *methylenebis(oxy)* is a **single** substituent; without them the name is ambiguous. | Always write *locant‑(methylenebis(oxy))* for a bridging group. |
