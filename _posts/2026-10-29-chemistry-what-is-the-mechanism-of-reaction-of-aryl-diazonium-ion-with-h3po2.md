### 1. Restatement of the Problem

When an aryl diazonium salt reacts with hypophosphorous acid ($\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$), the diazonium group ($\text{–N}_2^+$) is replaced by a hydrogen atom ($\text{–H}$), effectively reducing the diazonium ion to an unsubstituted or reduced aromatic ring (deamination). 

The student's confusion is entirely valid: **How does $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$ act as a source of hydride ($\text{H}^-$) or a hydrogen atom ($\text{H}^\bullet$) when its name and formula imply it is an acid that should release protons ($\text{H}^+$)?**

This solution explains the radical chain mechanism step-by-step to show how the hydrogen is ultimately transferred to the benzene ring.

### 2. Step-by-Step Mechanism

The reaction of an aryl diazonium salt with $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$ does **not** proceed via a polar ionic mechanism involving a hydride ion ($\text{H}^-$). Instead, it proceeds via a **free-radical chain mechanism** facilitated by trace metal impurities (often copper or iron) present in the reaction vessel. 

Here is the complete step-by-step breakdown:

#### Step 1: Tautomerization of Hypophosphorous Acid ($\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$)
Hypophosphorous acid (phosphinic acid) exists in two tautomeric forms. While the major form has a $\text{P–OH}$ proton that makes it an acid, the minor active form has a $\text{P–H}$ bond that is susceptible to homolytic cleavage (splitting to form radicals):

$$\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{Phosphinic acid (active tautomer with P–H bond)}$$

#### Step 2: Initiation (Single Electron Transfer via trace metals)
Trace amounts of transition metal ions (like $\text{Cu}^{+}$ or $\text{Fe}^{2+}$) in the solution transfer a single electron to the aryl diazonium ion ($\text{Ar–N}_2^+$).

1. The aryl diazonium ion accepts one electron to form an aryl radical and nitrogen gas ($\text{N}_2$):
   $$\text{Ar–N}_2^+ + e^- \longrightarrow \text{Ar}^\bullet + \text{N}_2 \uparrow$$
   *(Note: The driving force here is the formation of exceptionally stable nitrogen gas).*

2. The metal ion (now oxidized) abstracts a hydrogen atom (or single electron plus proton) from hypophosphorous acid ($\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$), generating a phosphinoyl radical ($\text{H}_2\text{PO}_2^\bullet$):
   $$\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2 + \text{Metal}^{n+1} \longrightarrow \text{H}_2\text{PO}_2^\bullet + \text{Metal}^n + \text{H}^+$$

#### Step 3: Propagation (Hydrogen Atom Abstraction)
This is the step that directly answers your question. **The hydrogen is not delivered as a hydride ($\text{H}^-$), but rather as a hydrogen atom ($\text{H}^\bullet$) via a radical abstraction.**

The highly reactive aryl radical ($\text{Ar}^\bullet$) collides with a molecule of hypophosphorous acid ($\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$). It abstracts a hydrogen atom directly from the $\text{P–H}$ bond of the acid:

$$\text{Ar}^\bullet + \text{H–PH}_2\text{O}_2 \longrightarrow \text{Ar–H} + \text{H}_2\text{PO}_2^\bullet$$

* **Result:** The aryl radical becomes your desired reduced product ($\text{Ar–H}$), and a new phosphinoyl radical ($\text{H}_2\text{PO}_2^\bullet$) is created.

#### Step 4: Chain Propagation Continues
The newly formed phosphinoyl radical ($\text{H}_2\text{PO}_2^\bullet$) reacts with another molecule of the aryl diazonium salt ($\text{Ar–N}_2^+$). It reduces the diazonium ion, kicking off another $\text{N}_2$ gas molecule and regenerating an aryl radical along with a metaphosphoric acid derivative:

$$\text{H}_2\text{PO}_2^\bullet + \text{Ar–N}_2^+ \longrightarrow \text{Ar}^\bullet + \text{N}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{PO}_3^+ \text{ (or related oxidized phosphorus species)}$$

This cycle repeats until all reactants are consumed.

### 3. Final Answer

Hypophosphorous acid ($\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$) does **not** act as a hydride donor ($\text{H}^-$). Instead, the reaction proceeds via a **free-radical chain mechanism**. 

1. The aryl diazonium ion loses $\text{N}_2$ gas to form an aryl radical ($\text{Ar}^\bullet$).
2. This aryl radical abstracts a hydrogen atom ($\text{H}^\bullet$) directly from the $\text{P–H}$ bond of $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$, yielding the reduced aromatic product ($\text{Ar–H}$).
3. $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$ is oxidized in the process to a phosphorus(III) or phosphorus(V) byproduct, while the aromatic ring gets its replacement hydrogen.

### 4. Common Mistakes

* **Assuming Ionic Hydride Transfer:** Thinking that $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$ acts like $\text{NaBH}_4$ or $\text{LiAlH}_4$ to deliver an $\text{H}^-$ ion. Remember, phosphorus-based reducing agents in this context operate via radical pathways, not polar nucleophilic hydride transfers.
* **Ignoring Trace Metals:** Forgetting that this reaction is often catalyzed by radical initiators or trace transition metals in glassware/reagents that trigger the initial single-electron transfer.
* **Confusing Oxidation States:** Assuming $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$ is just a spectator acid because of its acidic protons. Its reducing power actually stems from the abstractable hydrogen attached directly to the phosphorus atom ($\text{P–H}$ bond).
